4. THE TEN BIGGEST GAPS, RANKED
1. **No offer or value layer on any image.** Allowed truths are never printed:
   - BUY2/BUY3/BUY4, NAIRA10, COD, free insured shipping, the gift box;
   - and the mechanic is inconsistent (code against automatic).

   Discount hooks are saturated in India, so the offer should not be the whole idea. Every batch should still carry one offer-led ad, plus an offer band or sticker on the rest. Confirm the live mechanic before printing it.
2. **The thumb-stop hierarchy is inverted.**
   - The jewellery is often under 5% of the frame and type is tiny; the price is the smallest element.
   - Leaders run the hero product at 30–50% of the frame, a headline you can read at thumbnail size (about 8–12% of frame height), a legible price, and a CTA.
   - This hits the colour and crazy3 sets hardest (about 110 ads).
3. **A monotone visual world with no ownable brand system.** It is all warm cream, soft window light, three-quarter tabletop and the same worn styling.
   - Missing camera grammar: top-down grids, an eye-level hero, extreme macro, direct flash, coloured gels (only crazy10 has them), motion such as pouring or falling, in-hand POV, a mirror or phone-camera look.
   - Missing brand system: a fixed logo lockup position, one signature brand colour, one recurring device.
4. **No bundles, stacks or gift sets.** The one-product law blocks every AOV story, even though BUY2/BUY3/BUY4 exist. Missing:
   - wrist stacks, ear stacks and necklace layers made only from products with 20+ in stock, generated together at true size;
   - "Buy 3, 20% off" set cards;
   - the Naira gift box for Diwali and wedding gifting.
5. **No proof.** Zero reviews, ratings, sales counts or "most-loved" marks. These need verified data first: a read-only Shopify pull of reviews and orders, with nothing invented.
6. **The explainer archetypes are missing:**
   - callouts or anatomy (clasp, pavé link, adjustable band, shell pearl);
   - a size/how-to card (25 mm hoop on the ear; ring adjustable where listed; necklace length on the body);
   - demonstration and before/after (bare neckline → styled; clasp front versus back).

   These answer the objections a ₹999–₹2,399 buyer has: size, fit, how it closes, what it is made of.
7. **Copy mechanics are too narrow.**
   - Puns and generic occasion lines dominate, and the occasion lines have measured weakest.
   - The proven "X, not Y" craft truth is shrunk to a subline instead of the headline.
   - Problem→solution (X10), question hooks, numbers ("1 chain, 5 outfits"), Hinglish and direct address are rare.
   - The "real" breach in crazy13 must be cleared.
8. **Human presence is thin and identical.** There is no styled outfit (saree or lehenga blouse in wedding season), no creator or UGC look, and no hand interaction beyond posed worn crops. Yet the creator reel ("Where is this from?", 11.47% CTR) and the on-model carousel are the account's strongest assets.
9. **Scale honesty and AI tells in the crazy set.** The oversized pieces, star glints, floating objects and post-shrink composites listed above break the honest-scale rule and look "AI".
   - Choose referents the generator keeps at true size: an ear, a wrist, a fingertip, a coin, a paperclip.
   - Generate worn shots directly at true size, and drop the frames that fail; do not resize them.
10. **Pattern-interrupt formats never tried:**
    - us-vs-them without a named rival (back hook against front toggle; "one chain against five outfits");
    - an equation (piece + piece = look);
    - a type-led colour-block poster;
    - native UI beyond the Toggle tests (notes app, chat, poll, receipt-style price breakdown using real numbers only);
    - carousel or checkerboard statics for products other than the Toggle.

5. OWNER DECISIONS FLAGGED (not designed into anything)
- **Waterproof / tarnish-free.**
  - toggle-link/build/BRIEF.md quotes the Toggle listing: "Surgical stainless steel, 18k PVD gold tone plated, waterproof, tarnish free".
  - The winners board pushes these claims, and wear demonstrations (shower, monsoon, gym, sea) would be much stronger with them.
  - Inside the rule, use only "stainless steel, PVD gold tone" where the listing says so, and show durability without claiming it. Whether to allow the claims for steel/PVD SKUs whose listing supports them is the owner's call.
- **Faces and creators.**
  - BRIEF.md bans faces in worn shots, yet the top CTR asset is a creator.
  - Decide: use real collab photos (Riya, Ananaya) with rights as statics, or allow generated faces.
- **Proof data.** Decide which verified review or sales figures may be printed.
- **Dhanteras positioning.** crazy28 sets plated huggies on plain gold coins under "Dhanteras, sorted.", which can read as a precious-gold purchase. Keep "gold tone" explicit, or retire the coin motif.
- **Open listing questions** (from earlier work): Pearl Circlet Studs (gold in the copy, silver in every photo); Toggle clasp drawn as a bar against an oval hoop.

5. THE 10 BIGGEST GAPS, RANKED
=====================================================

1. No offer anywhere.
   - What's missing: NAIRA10, BUY2/BUY3/BUY4, free insured shipping and COD are absent from all of about 400 files, and The Light set argues against offers.
   - How to close it: all of these are allowed and are the biggest conversion lever for ₹800–2,400 impulse buys in India. Build offer cards: the code as the headline, BUY3 sets, and a "COD · free insured shipping" strip.

2. No benefit headline.
   - What's missing: the winners lead with a 2–5 word benefit (57 of 200). Ours lead with colour names, tool names and hex codes.
   - How to close it, within the rules:
     - "Surgical steel. 18k gold tone."
     - "Adjustable 15–19cm."
     - "One chain. Every neckline."
     - "Turn, not a catch."

3. Product fidelity on the heroes.
   - What's wrong: the Toggle clasp is drawn wrong across about 20 cards, and the Heartbead looks like three different products.
   - How to close it: fix the reference before scaling to 500. Lock one approved photo per hero, check every frame against ref-clasp.jpg, and use real listing photos where the render keeps failing.

4. No proof.
   - What's missing: no reviews, ratings or order counts on any card (proof leads 20 of the 200 winners).
   - How to close it: needs verified real numbers from Shopify or the reviews app. Never invent.

5. Stock discipline.
   - What's wrong: about half the archive features pieces under 20 units.
   - How to close it: build only on Toggle, Woven, Prism and Heartbead (re-check live stock; the Huggies at 16 fail). Hold low-stock pieces for organic.

6. Bundles without a bundle mechanic.
   - What's wrong: stacks are priced at the full sum, which makes the basket look expensive.
   - How to close it: lay out a set (for example Toggle + Prism + Woven) with BUY2/BUY3 as the hook. This works with only the four 20+ SKUs.

7. Format monoculture.
   - What's wrong: one template carries about 80 cards. Us-vs-them, equation, before/after (a styling change, such as a plain kurta before and with the chain after, needs no claim), review card, collection grid, split screen and native phone-UI formats never appear.

8. Legibility at phone size.
   - What's wrong:
     - text takes 40–50% of the frame while the product often takes under 15%
     - spec type is about 6px on a phone
     - CTA, name and price sit in the stories' bottom zone
     - prices, names, logos and CTAs are duplicated
     - the logo disappears on light grounds
   - How to close it: let the product fill 50–70% of the frame, use three text elements at most, and drop the in-image button.

9. Camera monotony and unfinished worn assets.
   - What's wrong: one macro recipe throughout. Flash, top-down grid, phone/POV, true-size scale heroes and oversized-on-colour shots are missing. The keep-it-on plates, the best worn photography, were never finished with a single line.

10. No learning loop and no single brand look.
    - What's wrong: every set self-scores 9.0–9.5 and none has been tested, so nothing says which device sells. The palette drifts across about eight systems, mostly off the brand deck.
    - How to close it: run every batch of five as a test with one variable changed. Add one fixed brand anchor to every card (the deck's sage or blush, a larger wordmark).

OWNER DECISIONS: where a winner pattern needs a claim the house rules ban
=====================================================

1. Water claims.
   - The board's top Naira suggestions are "Waterproof. Tarnish-free." said big, the equation "Surgical steel + 18k gold tone PVD = waterproof, tarnish-free", and before/after a week of showers.
   - Our strongest existing demonstrations depend on the same claim: night-shift/02-the-test, 10-the-answer A1/A2, red-room R1/R4 and all of keep-it-on.
   - Shower, pool and sweat imagery implies the claim even without the words.
   - The owner should choose:
     - (a) allow the claim on SKUs whose listing states it, or
     - (b) allow the imagery but not the words, or
     - (c) drop water imagery entirely.
   - Until that is decided, the keep-it-on plates and the Test and Answer sets cannot run.

2. "Is it real gold? NO." (night-shift/10-the-answer/feed/A3-gold).
   - Honest objection-handling, but "real" is banned.
   - A compliant rewrite such as "Is it gold? 18k gold tone on surgical steel." loses some punch.

3. Us versus them.
   - It is strongest on water and tarnish (the board suggests "PVD vs plated").
   - Within the rules it can compare only on true, checkable facts (surgical steel base, 18k gold tone, price, COD, free insured shipping). The owner should approve any comparative claim.

4. Proof numbers. The owner needs to supply or approve a real order or review source.

5. Face policy. Decide whether a partial face (lips, chin, profile) is allowed. The board's "face, product, one line" pattern (24 of 200) uses faces, and our checks currently ban them.
