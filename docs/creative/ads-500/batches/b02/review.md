## Fix target for the next batch

**Stop power, through the piece.** It is the one dimension that fell (7.63 → 7.00), both critics name it, and it is where every
winner anchor beats us. At 200 px the piece, not the hair, body, ground or headline, must be the shape that lands in half a
second.
- **Size.** Every worn frame puts the key detail at **18% or more of the Feed width**. Use a single-ear crop about 12 cm across
  for earrings (a 25 mm hoop or a Circlet stud is then about 21%), or a 9–10 cm hand crop for rings. The pair-at-the-nape framing
  caps out at 13%.
- **Position.** The piece sits in the central column of the frame (target x 300–780 on the Feed, never outside x 216–864), not
  at the edges. Write the composition around the model's centring: subject at 40–60% of the height, headline above, card below.
- **Contrast.** Gold goes on a ground darker or cooler than gold (oxblood, cocoa, ink, deep teal, sage-deep, sky); clear,
  silver and green stones on dark or flat saturated grounds. Never gold on apricot or another same-hue ground.
- **Gate it before layout.** On the 200 px sheet, measure the piece's share of the width and its colour difference from the
  ground behind it. Drop anything where the hair, head or headline is the first shape to land.
- **Placement by anatomy, not percentages.** Get position from crop edges that cut named body parts and from a camera behind or
  above the body, as in b02-1.
- **One honest surprise ships.**
- **Acceptance:** both critics name the product in half a second on the 200 px blind sheet, and at least one ad beats the winner
  anchor with at least one critic.

## Prompting learnings

1. **A "SCALE, the most important instruction" clause with a visible anatomical referent fixed worn scale on a simple piece.**
   For Woven it named three ratios (each hoop about 40% of the ear's height, four hoops across the neck, one eighth of the frame
   width) plus never-limits. Try 1 was 1.7× true; try 2 is 1.1–1.2×. The same device on the Prism wrist ("one eighth of the
   wrist's width, never one sixth or one fifth", "twelve stones edge to edge") moved pitch ÷ wrist from 0.178 to 0.150.
   Cross-check worn scale against a verified true-size plate, not only nominal mm.
2. **The same device breaks a multi-part, asymmetric piece.** On the Circlet, "stud as tall as the earlobe, green stone half the
   lobe" kept the green stone true and inflated the circlet, turned the round pearl into a teardrop and added a sixth clear stone.
   For composite pieces, lock the internal ratio first ("circlet about 1.3× the green stone's width, never twice; the big pearl
   is a round bead") and size only the outline against the body.
3. **Tight-crop clauses do not keep eyes out.** The model ignored "the hair covers the top of the ear" twice and framed about
   15 cm when asked for 11. Use a view where the face is physically hidden (straight or three-quarter from behind).
4. **Changing a ring's pose away from its reference pose breaks complex settings.** Verdant Eternity's new pose collapsed the
   S-wave. Keep the reference pose and set size by framing or camera distance; for tall subjects in 9:16, lock the height share.
5. **Subjects still grow 1.3–1.75× past the size asked, and placement percentages still do not move them.** Ask for about 70%
   of the size wanted, describe placement physically, and build the template around a centred subject. A diagonal arm appeared
   once "horizontal" was no longer the first geometric phrase.
6. **Measure acceptance ratios from the reference photos, not nominal sizes.** Woven diameter ÷ braid is about 4.1 (not 5);
   the Prism pitch is about 8.1 mm (1.62 × 5 mm, not 7.5); Verdant Eternity oval ÷ opening is 0.19 (not 0.30).
7. **Verifier calibration.** For worn scale use the referent ranges (wrist 5.5–6.5 cm, ear about 6 cm) and report the central
   estimate, not the most favourable body. b02-4 passed at an assumed 50–56 mm wrist and fails at 6 cm.
8. **What held:** direct black-and-white selective colour (zero coloured pixels outside the stud, twice); the brushed finish;
   braid, latch and disc; the Prism colours and halos. The L3 gel rim renders as a flat coloured stroke unless asked for "a soft
   faint glow that fades into the skin".
9. **Water on the piece reads as hardware.** A bead hanging from the hoop reads as a jump ring at phone size. Add "no water bead
   or droplet on the jewellery itself; no droplet hangs from the bottom of the hoop".
10. **A blue floor tints brushed gold** grey-green in its lower half even with a never-clause. Use a warmer or darker ground, or
    name a gold bounce card as the only light from below.
11. **Credits: 2 per picture** (20 for b02's 10 pictures), every job served as `nano_banana_2`.
