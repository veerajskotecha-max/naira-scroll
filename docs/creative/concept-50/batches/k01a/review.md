### V2 expert worst issues

- **perf1:** The headline says weight but the picture shows a sight test: nobody is weighing anything, so 'Bhaari hai' has no visual proof and lens plus scale are two competing ideas. Shoot the bracelet pooled in the aunty's open palm, mid-heft, with the full strand of eight pearls visible. Drop the lens callout.
- **perf2:** The prop and the claim pull apart. The headline and the drawn balance sell weight ('Bhaari hai, achha hai', 27.6 g), but the photograph shows a reading-glasses inspection, which proves looks, not heft. The caption also says 'weighed in her palm' while showing a balance. Make the picture prove the headline: the bracelet draped over the aunty's open palm. Or put this line on a weight-led frame and keep this lens frame for the 'no two alike' line.
- **craft1:** The headline sells weight but the picture proves looking, so the claim and the image pull apart. Move the 27.6 g scale up as the proof beat right under 'Bhaari hai. Achha hai.' and cut the lens callout and eye-chart numbers, so one idea (heft equals quality) owns the frame.
- **craft2:** The picture proves the wrong claim. The headline sells weight ('Bhaari hai. Achha hai.' plus 27.6 g), but the photo is the lens inspection, built for the look-up-close claim. The copy says 'weighed in her palm' while the graphic shows a balance scale. Shoot the bracelet pooled in the aunty's cupped palm, or on the tarazu pan, and drop the lens callout.
- **truth1:** The headline sells weight, but the photo shows a lens inspection, and the 'weighed in her palm' proof is never shown: a balance drawing (with roundish, almost uniform pearl icons) sits on top of a lens photo, so the two devices compete. Show the bracelet in the aunty's palm or on a jeweller's balance, or pair this headline with a picture that proves it.
- **truth2:** The headline is about weight ('Bhaari hai. Achha hai.') but the picture shows a test through the lens, so nothing in the picture proves 27.6 g. Either show the bracelet resting heavy in the aunty's palm, or pair this picture with the lens headline.
