  - It is about a quarter of her neck's width. Correct is about a sixth, which k04a R had.
  - The ring reads about **33 mm** on a listing that says 22 mm.
  - This is not scale play. The brief says "SCALE, the most important instruction: the necklace is fine against the body". The camp has no giant-piece joke, and the size line says 22 mm. A buyer would receive a clasp two-thirds the size shown, and the store has no return policy. **Do not ship it as it is.**
- **Cause:** the prompt contradicts itself. "The field of view is about 12 cm, so the ring spans about a fifth of the frame width" cannot hold in a frame that shows the bun, both ears and both hands, which is about 20 cm. The model kept the frame fraction (the ring is 0.17 of the width), drew the body at life size, and so grew the ring.
- **Fixable by one regeneration:** yes. Change only the SCALE clause (see Redo fix).

## Redo fix (take A2: A's prompt word for word, with these clause changes)

1. **SCALE: replace** "the field of view is about 12 cm, so the ring spans about a fifth of the frame width and the bar about two thirds of the ring" **with:**
   > the field of view is about 20 cm, from just outside one ear to just outside the other, so the ring spans only about one ninth of the frame width (about 11 percent, never more than one eighth) and the bar about two thirds of the ring.
2. **SCALE: replace** "The ring is about as wide as two paperclip links laid end to end" **with:**
   > The ring is exactly as wide as two paperclip links laid end to end, never three: each paperclip link is half the ring's width long, and the chain stays exactly as fine as it is.
3. **SCALE: add after the fingertip sentence:**
   > The ring is about one and a half fingertips wide, never two or three fingertips; last time the ring came out one and a half times too big (three links long and a quarter of her neck's width) while the chain was right: draw the ring smaller, never the chain or the hands.
4. **Construction: add after "The bar is dead straight, with one single row of tiny clear stones."**
   > The chain's last link passes straight through the bar's own round eye, with no extra small ring between them.
5. **COMPOSITION: keep as is.** The bun opener worked this time (the clasp moved up from 57% to 46%). A smaller ring will lift the ring's lower edge to about 50% down, which clears the Story card.
6. **Keep word for word:**
   - the reference sentences and "The clasp is posed exactly as in reference 2 ... never its framing or its size";
   - the lock and its must-stay clause;
   - the listing's-own-ring clause and the gate-by-clock sentence;
   - the two-ends topology;
   - the fingers resting on the lying chain, and "No finger covers ...";
   - the Peter Pan collar in deep shadow;
   - LIGHT ("never rays, never star-shaped glints") and GRADE;
   - the face gate, "No water, no droplets", the no-text clause and the one-product clause.
   - Every one of these delivered in this take.
