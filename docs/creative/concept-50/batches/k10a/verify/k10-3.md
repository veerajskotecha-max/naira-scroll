## Verdict: both takes FAIL. Choice: none (redo take A's composition)

**Take A** is the campaign picture: a green moon in the sky and its reflection in the lake. It is 95% right:
- every part is in the right count and order;
- the mirror is right, with the lower stud the true 180-degree twin;
- the lake is painted, the colour is right, and there is no text or face.

It fails on three counts, all fixable by one regeneration:
1. **Invented spike.** On the upper stud only, the clear-stone star's lower-left point is stretched into a second faceted gold segment. This spike reaches out and fuses with the pavilion's roof, which is painted gilt where they meet. It does not exist on the real piece or on the lower stud. The ad's whole claim is that the two studs are exact mirror images, so this spike is the one place where the tracing proof visibly fails.
2. **Studs too big and too low.** Each stud is 32% of the frame width (the brief says 19%), and the pair runs from 25.7% to 67.5% of the frame height (the brief says 26-53%). The camp's Feed grid then fits at no P, and in the Story the F2 card covers the lower stud.
3. **Green stone off the 3 px gate.** In the fold test the circlet lands within about 3 Feed px, but the lower green stone's rim is 5-8 Feed px off.

**Take B** is faithful: both studs are right, they mirror correctly, and it lays out cleanly in the Feed. But it **loses the card's idea**:
- It is the plan's hedge. At 200 px it reads as a lovely pair of earrings above a painted lake, not as reflections.
- Its own proof device cannot be done honestly. The right stud is a true mirror of the left, but it is turned about 9.6 degrees in the plane, as in ref 03. So no single fold maps one stud onto the other, and the sideways fold misses the big pearl by about 30 Feed px.
- Crazy 5, stop200 6.

B is a usable fallback if the redo fails: drop the fold motion, keep a static axis, and re-flow the Story card.
