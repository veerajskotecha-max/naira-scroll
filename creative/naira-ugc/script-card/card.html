03  /  THE SHOOT

What to bring. What to shoot.

Shoot in this order — dry setups first, wet last, so nothing has to be dried twice. Three takes of everything, five of each unbroken proof.

What to have ready

The dead pile
Your own old jewellery — blackened chains, green rings, anything discoloured. In a small dish you can tip out. This is frame one of the ad, and one of the chains needs a visibly kinked or gapped link for the close‑up.
Both Naira pieces, clean
Wipe with a microfibre cloth before every setup. At macro distance, one fingerprint is the whole shot.
A running tap
Kitchen or bathroom, with good light on it. Record the water sound live — it is the best audio in the reel.
Tripod or phone stand
Non‑negotiable for the macros. At that distance the depth of field is a few millimetres and breathing moves the focus.
A white card
Held opposite the window to bounce light back into the shadows. A sheet of A4 works.
A quiet room, 10 minutes
Sound only: the pile clattering, the lobster clasp clicking, the chain poured onto a hard surface. Phone 10cm away, nothing else playing.
Phone settings — once, before anything

60fps so slow motion stays an option  ·  tap to focus, then press and hold to lock AE/AF — focus hunting mid‑take ruins most jewellery footage  ·  no flash, ever  ·  macros handheld with a slight drift, anything with your face on a tripod. Movement makes metal look alive; stillness makes a face look honest.

The two things that are not negotiable

1 · #Ad goes in the top third of the frame, from the first second, and holds for nine seconds. Top third, not bottom — Instagram covers the bottom with its own buttons. Required for gifted collaborations, not just paid ones, and the brand carries the liability.

2 · Neither proof shot may be cut. If there is an edit between the twist and the release, or between the water and the reveal, the proof is worth nothing and the ad is worse than having no proof at all.
