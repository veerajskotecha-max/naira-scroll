The whole idea

Two gold chains, side by side, near‑identical. One hand twists both. One kinks and stays kinked; ours springs back straight. The only words on screen are “one of these is ₹2,149” — then: guess which one. The viewer has to answer a question before they can leave, and because it is a test rather than a claim, it works with the sound off.

03  /  THE SHOOT

What to bring. What to shoot.

Shoot in this order — dry setups first, wet last, so nothing has to be dried twice. Three takes of everything, eight of the hook.

What to have ready

One old gold‑tone chain
Your own, cheap, and close enough to the Charm Box Chain that they read alike at a glance. It needs a visibly open or gapped link for the macro. No brand name or logo in frame — we compare to a chain, never to a competitor.
Both Naira pieces, clean
Wipe with a microfibre cloth before every setup. At macro distance, one fingerprint is the whole shot.
A running tap
Kitchen or bathroom, with good light on it. Record the water sound live — it is the best audio in the reel.
Tripod or phone stand
Non‑negotiable for the macros. At that distance the depth of field is a few millimetres and breathing moves the focus.
A white card
Held opposite the window to bounce light back into the shadows. A sheet of A4 works.
A quiet room, 10 minutes
Sound only: the twist and spring‑back, the lobster clasp clicking, the chain poured onto a hard surface. Phone 10cm away, nothing else playing.
Phone settings — once, before anything

60fps so slow motion stays an option  ·  tap to focus, then press and hold to lock AE/AF — focus hunting mid‑take ruins most jewellery footage  ·  no flash, ever  ·  macros handheld with a slight drift, anything with your face on a tripod. Movement makes metal look alive; stillness makes a face look honest.

The two things that are not negotiable

1 · #Ad goes in the top third of the frame, from the first second, and holds for nine seconds. Top third, not bottom — Instagram covers the bottom with its own buttons. Required for gifted collaborations, not just paid ones, and the brand carries the liability.

2 · Neither the hook take nor the tap take may be cut. An edit between the twist and the spring‑back, or between the water and the reveal, makes the proof worth nothing — and the hook is the twist, so a cut there costs the whole ad.

Why the hook is a test and not a sentence

It survives the mute. A spoken hook is worth nothing to the majority who watch without sound. A demonstration is legible either way — and this one has a price on screen at 0.2 seconds.

It opens a loop the viewer has to close. “Guess which one” makes them answer before they can scroll. That is a stronger device than any adjective, and they leave knowing something they didn’t — which is what gets a reel saved rather than watched.

It argues construction. Your audit of 498 competitor creatives found nobody in this category argues craft. Square links. Surgical steel. Lobster clasp. Of ~1,500 active Indian jewellery ads on Meta this month, one ran a durability mechanism. One.

It never says anything ugly. Nothing is called cheap, nothing is shamed, and there is no insult to what she already owns — the other chain is simply open, stated as a fact about a thing in frame. The brand stays soft while the argument stays hard.

And nothing in it is invented. Every claim is either Naira’s own product copy or something the camera proves in a single unbroken take.
