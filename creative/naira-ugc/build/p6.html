Shooting rules that decide whether this works

  • Shoot the talking head on a tripod and the product macros handheld. Stability reads as credibility on a face; drift reads as life on metal. The opposite arrangement is exactly what makes UGC look fake.
  • One window, both setups. If the macros are in 5600K daylight and the face is under a 3200K bulb, no grade will match them without costing you gold accuracy. Shoot both in the same light.
  • Set a custom white balance from a grey card in that light and use the same value on every shot. A small warm subject often has no true neutral in frame, so auto-WB guesses differently shot to shot — that is precisely what produced the +25 to +80 skew spread in the current reel.
  • Never the built-in flash. If adding light, use CRI ≥95 LEDs — cheap LEDs put a green-yellow spike into gold that no grade fully removes.
  • 9:16 exaggerates horizontal movement. A pan that reads smooth in 16:9 is nauseating vertical. Keep lateral motion minimal or use a gimbal.
  • Twenty minutes of foley is the highest-value recording on the shoot (A15–A17). No library SFX will match your actual box and your actual clasp.

Music licensing — this will get the ad rejected

You cannot use Instagram’s trending-audio library in a paid ad. The moment a Reel is boosted or built as an ad, Meta fingerprints the audio against the commercial licensing database. Unlicensed audio means a rejected boost and a copyright flag; repeated flags reportedly restrict audio on all your ads.

Use: Meta Sound Collection (cleared, but Meta platforms only) or an Epidemic Sound / Artlist subscription if the asset also has to run on YouTube or your site.

And turn off Advantage+ “Music” and “Image animation” at ad level. Meta enables these by default and will lay an unbriefed bed over your VO.

Grade — correct, don’t style

  1. Neutralise every shot to the same white-balance reference first.
  2. Match exposure on the skin, not on the background. Then match black level.
  3. Only then apply one very light shared look. Trim it per shot — never treat a LUT as set-and-forget.
  4. Gold: pull saturation out of the orange range specifically while holding yellow; lift the highlight roll-off so speculars go to near-white rather than clipping orange. Brassy gold is the first tell of cheap jewellery imagery.
  5. Indian skin: sits slightly below the standard skin-tone line on the vectorscope. Grade to that, not to the printed line. Most LUTs are built for lighter Western skin and will push it pink or grey. Grade shadows, mids and highlights separately — never a global warm push, which turns already red-yellow skin orange fast.
  6. Ceiling: WB + exposure + a gentle contrast S + selective orange desaturation + a skin qualifier. No film emulation, no halation, no heavy vignette, no teal shadows. Over-polish costs you the format’s entire advantage.
  7. Target: shot-to-shot mean luminance within ±15 and R−B skew within ±8. The current reel is at ±52 and ±27.

Graphics

  • Name card: top third (y 400–650), left-aligned ragged right, 1.5–2.5s hold. If the ground behind it fails contrast, darken that region — never add a scrim box. That is your own rule and it is the right one.
  • Price chip: adjacent to the product in frame, never in a corner. 1.2–2.0s. Quiet — thin rule, brand type, the ₹ figure. No starburst, no red, no percentage. At demi-fine prices a discount-bin badge actively damages the category cue.
  • Never three text objects at once. Caption + name card + chip on one 1080-wide frame is the mistake the current reel makes.
  • Motion: hard cut-in on a VO beat or a foley hit. If animating, 6–8 frames of y-translate + opacity. No scale-bounce, no spring easing — that is the CapCut-default tell.
  • No persistent brand bug. Unwatermarked UGC reads as content, not advertising. Brand through the packaging in the unboxing shot, one spoken mention, and the end card. If a bug is politically required: ≤40px, 40% opacity, top-left at x=60 y=300, and only from halfway through.
  • End card: 1.5–2.5s over live product footage, never a flat colour slab. Minor motion on the CTA element. Music resolves — never cut to silence.
