06  /  CLAIMS & COMPLIANCEFix these before spend, not after

Four findings. Two are genuine exposure. I have checked each claim in the reel against your own live product pages.

Verified — the claim holds

“Two years plating guarantee on all of it” — supported

Every hero PDP carries “2-year plating assurance” alongside “7-day returns” and “₹150 insured shipping”. The claim is substantiated. But it is not on /jewellery, which is where the ad sends people. Your single strongest line is missing from the landing page it drives to. Put the assurance badge in the collection header.

Exposure — act on these

1 · The end card says WATERPROOF. Your FAQ does not.

Your own site: “It is waterproof-sealed for incidental contact such as rain or washing hands. Remove pieces before swimming, as chlorine and salt water degrade any plated finish over time.” The end card’s flat WATERPROOF is a broader claim than the page it links to. Established guidance for this category in India is to write water-resistant, or the demonstrable and much stronger “rain, washing up, monsoon — it stays on”, unless you hold a test result. Also note your BRAND.md territory two currently reads “Shower in it. Swim in it.” — “swim in it” directly contradicts your FAQ and should be struck from the territory list.

2 · No ASCI disclosure label anywhere in 33.4 seconds

If Riya was paid or gifted — barter counts — ASCI’s influencer guidelines require a disclosure label (#Ad, #Sponsored or #Collaboration) visible above the fold. For a video of this length the requirement is visible for one-third of the runtime — about 11 seconds here. There is none, and the advertiser carries the liability, not the creator. ASCI processed 1,409 influencer violations through Nov 2025; 94% were disclosure failures — it is overwhelmingly the thing that gets caught.

Fix: burn #Ad into the top third of the frame (not the bottom — Reels chrome covers it) from 0:00, and hold it 11s. Tag the ad as branded content in Meta.

3 · Drip-pricing risk when you start naming a price

The CCPA Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023 name thirteen patterns; drip pricing is quoting a figure the checkout then exceeds. You charge ₹150 insured shipping. The moment the VO says “₹1,199”, say what it lands at — “₹1,199 plus ₹150 insured delivery” or “₹1,349 delivered”. This costs you nothing and removes the exposure entirely. Penalties under the Consumer Protection Act 2019 run to ₹50 lakh and endorsement bans up to three years.

Also: never write “18K gold” — always “18K gold-plated”. BIS hallmarking has been mandatory for 14K/18K/22K gold since 2021 (9K added July 2025) and applies to solid gold only. Your Woven Gold Hoops PDP currently lists Finish as 18k gold. That is a wording fix on the site, and a hard rule for every script. Note too that the Cushion Halo Ring is rhodium-finished, not gold — no script may call it gold.

4 · Reviews appear to be pooled across SKUs

The Cushion Halo Ring PDP shows a 4.9★ review describing “the blush cluster ring”; the Woven Gold Hoops PDP shows one describing “the pastel zircone bracelet”. If reviews are shared across products, that is a misleading-review risk under the same CCPA guidelines, and it will read as fake to any viewer who clicks through from an ad that just told them to trust an unknown brand. Worth correcting before you spend behind trust-led creative.

Meta policy traps to keep out of every future script

  • No outcome guarantees. “Never tarnishes”, “lasts forever”. Write “316L steel core — it won’t rust” or “worn daily for 60 days”, both substantiable.
  • No personal attributes. “If your skin turns green” is fine. “If you have sensitive skin” is a rejection.
  • No named competitors. “vs the ₹18,000 one” is safe; “the [Brand] dupe” is not.
  • No false urgency. Your BRAND.md already says this. Five units per SKU is real scarcity and a genuine asset — but only state it when it is true on the day.
  • Turn off Advantage+ creative enhancements at ad level — specifically Music and Image animation. Meta enables these by default and will lay an unbriefed music bed over your VO and warp product edges.
The unresolved strategic contradiction — your call, not mine

Your BRAND.md, built from 498 live competitor creatives, concludes: “Discounting is not the entry fee… the brand that carries an offer in 0% of copy holds the best median impression rank of six, and the one that discounts in 80% ranks second worst.”

The live site is 100% discount-led: every SKU carries a struck-through MRP (32–62% off), and a scrolling bar repeats 10% OFF YOUR FIRST ORDER · USE CODE NAIRA10. The ad’s end card doubles down with the same code. Whatever the merits, the research you commissioned and the store you built are arguing opposite positions, and the creative is currently following the store.

My recommendation, which lets you have both: run price-led on cold, code-led on retargeting. On cold traffic say the real number — “₹1,199, COD available” — because a real price is a claim about value and a percentage is a claim about desperation. Save NAIRA10 for people who already know you. Of the ~1,500 active Indian jewellery ads pulled, near-every one is a discount or a price ladder. Being the one that isn’t is the cheapest differentiation available to you.
