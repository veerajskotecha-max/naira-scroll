What is evidence and what is craft opinion

Two figures in this document carry real statistical weight: Motion’s Creative Benchmarks 2026 (578,750 creatives, 6,015 advertiser accounts, $1.29bn of Meta spend, Sep 2025–Jan 2026) and the WCAG 2.2 contrast thresholds. The 52-account CAC comparison behind the negative-hook recommendation is the best decision-grade evidence found for hook family. Everything else — caption-style lifts, foley lifts, end-card lifts, speed-ramp effects — is vendor-reported without published methodology and should be treated as a craft heuristic, not a fact. I have not dressed those up as certainties.

Three things are worth knowing are not established:

  • Meta publishes no safe-zone pixel table. Every number in §5 and §10 is a conservative union of four third-party reverse-engineerings that disagree by up to 270px. Preview on a real phone before launch.
  • Meta publishes no LUFS target. −14 LUFS / −1 dBTP is inferred industry consensus. What is confirmed is that Meta uses xHE-AAC with dynamic loudness management, and that phone speakers have almost no response below 200 Hz — so mid-range clarity at 1–4 kHz matters more than any LUFS figure. Mix so the VO is intelligible on a phone speaker at half volume in a noisy room.
  • “85% watch without sound” is a 2016 publisher self-report, never confirmed by Meta, with no credible 2025–26 replacement. The operating rule holds regardless: every claim in the voiceover must also exist as a caption or a graphic, and the proof demo must be self-evident with the sound off. Treat sound as upside, never as load-bearing.
