## The correction that forced the rewrite

The client said the brown stone is **not tiger eye**. Checking the product photograph at zoom
confirms it: the stone is **faceted and translucent with a domed rose-cut top**. Tiger eye is cut
as a smooth cabochon because faceting destroys the chatoyant sheen, and there is no cat's-eye
stripe anywhere on it.

Three things on the ring PDP are wrong:

| The page says | The photograph shows |
|---|---|
| "a natural **tiger eye**… opaque, banded, with a soft moving sheen" | a faceted, translucent honey-brown stone, domed rose-cut top |
| "**brilliant-cut** zircone" | both stones are flat-topped **rose cuts** |
| "This **solid gold** band" — in the meta description, so it shows in every shared link | 18K gold-tone **plated** over surgical steel |

**The script now names no mineral at all** and describes only colour, size, shape and setting —
what the camera proves. The product page still needs fixing separately.

## The script — 35s, 82 words

> "Two people have asked if this ring is broken." … "It isn't. It's meant to look like that." …

## What changed from the previous draft, and why

1. **The hook has other people in it.** "I nearly sent this ring back" was only about her.
   "Two people have asked" puts a third party in the room — the reported-speech family the client
   picked out, and the thing that gets a Reel forwarded in a DM rather than commented on.
2. **The closer shuts the loop.** *"Only if you want people to ask"* answers the first line: the
   objection becomes the selling point, with nobody claiming anything. The verdict stays uneven,
   which is the entire credibility of a review.
3. **Every spec left her mouth.** The previous draft said *bezel-set, surgical steel, two years on
   the plating, seven-day returns* out loud — six informational beats in a row, which is precisely
   the "salesmanship" that lowers emotional response. **Those are now on-screen chips.** The
   sound-off viewer still gets every number.
4. **The necklace got a joke instead of a footnote.** *"Nobody's questioned the necklace"* does in
   four words what a feature list can't — it establishes that the two pieces are different
   propositions, and it earns the uneven verdict.

## Deliberately absent: the pairing offer

A review that pitches a bundle stops being a review. **Put `BUY2` in the caption, not in her
mouth.** (That also sidesteps the unresolved 10% vs 20% question.)

## Before she records

- **How long has she had them?** The hook says two people have *already asked*, which only works if
  she has worn the ring out of the house. Nothing claims a duration. If it has been days, open with
  *"I've had these about a week."*
- **If nobody has actually asked**, use the alternate: *"I nearly sent this ring back."* Everything
  from 4.0s is unchanged; the closer becomes *"Ring only if you like things that argue."*
- A **22s cold cut** is on page 2 — six beats, ring only, no new footage.
