# Ananya — variations D, E, F

Companion to `VARIATIONS.md` (A, B, C). Same two SKUs, same creator, same
single shoot. Source: `w.html` + `r2.css` → `../NAIRA_Ananya_Variations_D-E-F.pdf`
(5 pages).

## Why three more, and why not three more of the same

Six creatives are only six if they read as six. A, B and C used the three
opening devices with the strongest evidence behind them — reported speech,
silent demonstration, declaration. D, E and F use the three strongest ones
left, and each argues a different *thing*:

| | Script | Device | Runtime | Argues | Placement |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D | Online, everything is the same size | observation | 27s / 63 w | size | saves |
| E | Don't buy this ring | direct contradiction | 31s / 74 w | fit / who it's for | cold |
| F | I never wear the expensive stuff | confession, mid-action | 22s / 50 w | value | value |

None repeats another's job. Run **E against A on cold traffic** —
contradiction vs. objection-first are the two strongest families measured,
same product, same creator, so the read is clean. **B and D** go to saves,
**C and F** to warm and festive.

## D — "Online, everything is the same size"

The one objection nobody in this category answers: *how big is it really*.
BRAND.md puts it at 42% of shoppers judging size from the image alone, and it
is the single largest driver of jewellery returns.

Every number spoken is lifted straight off the PDP — 17mm inner diameter,
15mm baroque shell pearl, 1.5mm snake chain. **Check them against the physical
pieces before she records**, because the same PDP is wrong about the stones.

Two production rules decide whether this works:

1. The referent must be **in the same frame** as the object.
2. There must be **no cut** between the object and the thing it's measured
   against. A cut destroys the proof.

Closes on *"Now you know what's arriving."*

Originally drafted with *"The stones are chunkier than the photos make them
look"* — an unverifiable comparative claim. Replaced with *"Each stone is
about a fingernail wide"*, which is checkable in frame.

## E — "Don't buy this ring"

Direct contradiction ran 25–40% under the benefit-led baseline on cold
prospecting across the 52-account DTC set — the largest effect of any hook
family measured, and the one A–C left on the table. It works because
disqualifying people is the most expensive-looking thing a brand can do.

*"The two stones don't match each other, let alone your earrings… It's not a
stacking ring either. It goes on a bare hand."* Then it flips: *"If you want
one odd thing people ask about — this is it."*

**The risk is tonal, not legal.** If Ananya plays it as a wink it becomes
reverse psychology and the script collapses. She has to mean it.

Note it also obeys the brand's own styling instruction — *"wear it on its own,
with the rest of the hand kept bare"* — on camera, which is a small piece of
proof most brands never bother to give.

## F — "I never wear the expensive stuff"

Confession, opened mid-action: she is already sorting through her own drawer
as she says the line. *"It lives in here. These two are on me."* The viewer
does the arithmetic — cost-per-wear made emotional instead of numerical.

Shortest and cheapest of all six: one drawer, one cut, two macros. Closes on
*"Buy the one you'll actually wear."* — seven words that make the value case
with no percentage anywhere.

**It is the only one of the six that still works word-for-word when BUY2
expires**, because it never names a discount, a comparison or a competitor.

The thing to get right is that the drawer must be real — her jewellery,
genuinely nicer things, no props and no other brand's box in frame.

## Never say — all six

- "Tiger eye", "natural stone", "real stone", "the light moves on it"
- "Brilliant-cut" — both stones are flat-topped rose cuts
- "Solid gold" or "18K gold" — it is 18K gold-**tone plated** over surgical steel
- "Pearl" alone — it is a **shell pearl**
- "Waterproof" · "only a few left" · "link in bio" · any rival's name

`#Ad` top third, frame one, for roughly a third of each runtime. The brand
carries the liability, not the creator.

## What none of the six does, and why

**None mentions the pairing offer.** The site says `BUY2` = 10% and the brief
said 20%; until that's settled, a spoken percentage is a promise the bag may
not keep. It belongs in the caption, where it can be changed in ten seconds
without a reshoot.

**None claims wear time.** A says two people have *already asked*, C says she
*bought* them, F says she *never wears* the expensive things — all three need
her to actually mean them. **D and E are true on day one regardless**, which
makes them the safest two to shoot first if the pieces only just arrived.
