THE SCRIPT · 35s · 82 WORDS

Two people have asked if this ring is broken

Tell it, don’t perform it · every spec is a chip on screen, never a line in her mouth
0.0 [no voice] Turning the ring on her hand, looking at it. Product on screen in frame one. #Ad
0.4 “Two people have asked if this ring is broken.” To camera, deadpan, slightly amused. The word “broken” lands at 2.2 seconds. Then a beat — let them look at it and decide for themselves.
4.0 “It isn’t. It’s meant to look like that.” Cut on “meant” to the tightest macro of the shoot — both stones filling the frame. The turn the whole reel is built on.
7.3 “Different colour, different size, different shape.” Three tiny cuts on the three words if you can — colour, then scale, then outline. Let the viewer verify each one.
9.8 “The gold’s built around each stone separately.” Slow rotate so the two bezels read as two different shapes. One soft key, one small hard glint — without the glint, gold renders flat. 18K GOLD-PLATED · SURGICAL STEEL
12.7 “It’s open at the back, so it fits six to eight.” Show the open back, then slide it on. The honest negative and the sizing answer in one line. OPEN BACK · FITS US 6–8
17.1 “Nobody’s questioned the necklace.” Hard cut to the lariat at her neck. Four words, and the joke does all the work — the contrast between the two pieces is the review.
18.9 “There’s a slider. I’ve worn it three lengths.” Fingers moving the slider, then two quick cuts at two different lengths.
22.1 “Big pearl’s a shell pearl — it says so on the page.” Macro on the baroque drop, turning. Visibly irregular. “It says so on the page” is the second honest beat — she is praising them for the disclosure, not excusing it. SHELL PEARL 2-YEAR PLATING ASSURANCE
26.9 “Twenty forty-nine and fifteen ninety-nine. Free delivery.” Both worn, plain top, daylight. Price chips beside each piece, never in a corner. ₹2,049 ₹1,599 FREE DELIVERY · 7-DAY RETURNS
29.8 “Necklace, easily. Ring only if you want people to ask.” Small smile. The closer calls the hook back and flips it — the thing that made it look broken is now the reason to buy it. Then 1.2s end card over live footage, music resolving. ↓ SHOP NOW
The hook now has other people in it
“I nearly sent this ring back” was only about her. “Two people have asked” puts a third party in the room — which is the reported-speech family you picked out, and the thing that gets a Reel forwarded in a DM rather than commented on.
The closer now shuts the loop
“Only if you want people to ask” answers the first line. The objection becomes the selling point without anyone claiming anything — and the verdict stays uneven, which is the entire credibility of a review.
Every spec left her mouth
The last draft said bezel-set, surgical steel, two years on the plating, seven-day returns out loud — six informational beats in a row. Those are now chips on screen. On-screen text, hard CTAs and fact stacks measurably lower emotional response; the sound-off viewer still gets every number.
The necklace got a joke instead of a footnote
“Nobody’s questioned the necklace” does in four words what a feature list can’t: it tells you the two pieces are different propositions, and it earns the uneven verdict at the end.

Never say

  • “Tiger eye”, “natural stone”, “real stone” — the photograph supports none of them
  • “The light moves on it” — that is chatoyancy, and this stone hasn’t got it
  • “Brilliant-cut” — both stones are flat-topped rose cuts
  • “Solid gold” or “18K gold” — it is 18K gold-tone plated over surgical steel
  • “Pearl” alone — it is a shell pearl, and saying so is half the point of the reel
  • “Waterproof” · “only a few left” · “link in bio” · any rival’s name
#Ad in the top third from frame one, held about twelve seconds. A review reel without disclosure is the most reported format there is, and the brand carries the liability, not the creator.

The one thing to settle before she records

How long has she had them? The hook says two people have already asked — which only works if she has worn the ring out of the house. Nothing in this script claims a duration, deliberately. If it has been days rather than weeks, that is fine: open with “I’ve had these about a week” and the rest stands. A first-impressions review is completely credible; an implied long-term one is what gets a creator taken apart in her own comments.

And if nobody has actually asked, say so and use the other version: “I nearly sent this ring back.” Everything from 4.0s on is unchanged. The reel still works — it just loses the callback at the end, and the closer becomes “Ring only if you like things that argue.”
