SCRIPT 01  ·  NAIRA PETITE  ·  ANANYA

Someone asked me
where this is from.

26 seconds
62 words
one take, then cutaways
Clean room · window light
Hook  ·  frame one
“Someone asked me where this is from. Twice, this week.”
Ring already on her hand, held loosely — not presented. Straight to camera, no smile. The ring must be visible in the very first frame, or the line has nothing to land on. #Ad in the top third from here.
1
“It’s this ring. Two stones, flat on top — and they don’t match each other.”
Macro, both stones in one frame, slow rotate so the two colours read as different.
2
“That’s the whole thing. It’s meant to look a bit odd.”
Back to her. Ring on the hand with nothing else on it.
3
“It’s open at the back, so it just fits. Six to eight.”
Show the open back, then slide it on. One unbroken move.
4
“This is the other one. The pearl’s uneven — no two are the same shape.”
Baroque pearl held close, turned once.
5
“There’s a slider, so it’s short with a collar and long with a kurta.”
Fingers on the slider, one quick length change on camera.
6
“Two thousand forty-nine, and fifteen ninety-nine. Free delivery.”
Both worn, daylight. Price chips beside each piece.
Close
“So if someone asks you — now you know.”
Small shrug, then stop. 1.2s end card over live footage, never over a freeze. SHOP NOW.

Why this one

It never says “buy”. Somebody else already wanted it — she is only answering. That is the single most reliable opening in the whole set, and it is the one you picked yourself.

Never say

“Tiger eye” · “natural stone” · “real pearl” · “solid gold” or “18K gold” · “waterproof” · “only a few left” · any percentage off

SCRIPT 02  ·  NAIRA PETITE  ·  ANANYA

Don’t buy this ring.

28 seconds
66 words
straight to camera
Clean room · window light
Hook  ·  frame one
“Don’t buy this ring if you want it to match anything.”
Straight to camera, completely level — no wink, no smile. Ring on, hand up. She means it; that is the entire mechanism. #Ad in the top third from here.
1
“The two stones don’t match each other. One’s deeper, one’s lighter.”
Tightest macro of the film — both stones in one frame so the viewer sees she is right.
2
“It’s not a stacking ring either. It goes on a bare hand.”
Wide on the hand, genuinely nothing else on it.
3
“Open at the back, fits six to eight. So you’re not guessing your size.”
Open back, then slide it on. The one practical beat in the script.
4
“The necklace is the same idea. That pearl is uneven on purpose.”
Pearl close, turned once.
5
“Fifteen millimetres. And a slider, so you set the length.”
Pearl against her thumbnail — same frame, no cut — then the slider.
6
“Two thousand forty-nine. Fifteen ninety-nine. Free delivery, seven-day returns.”
Both worn, whole hand, daylight. Price chips beside each piece.
Close — the flip
“If you want one odd thing people ask about — this is it.”
Back to camera. Say it like advice, not like a trick. The moment this reads as reverse psychology, the script dies. 1.2s end card over live footage.

Why this one

Telling people not to buy is the most expensive-looking thing a brand can do, so it reads as confidence rather than selling. Across 52 D2C accounts this family ran 25–40% under benefit-led hooks on cold traffic. Put your cold budget here.

Never say

“Tiger eye” · “natural stone” · “real pearl” · “solid gold” or “18K gold” · “waterproof” · “only a few left” · any percentage off

SCRIPT 03  ·  NAIRA PETITE  ·  ANANYA

Online, everything
looks the same size.

27 seconds
64 words
top-down, then hand
Clean room · window light
Hook  ·  frame one
“Online, everything looks the same size. So here are the actual sizes.”
Top-down on a plain surface, both pieces flat. Her hand enters and puts a ₹10 coin down next to them. That movement is the hook paid off. Nothing styled — this shot is evidence, not a photograph. #Ad in the top third from here.
1
“The ring is seventeen millimetres across the inside.”
Ring flat, coin in the same frame. Do not cut.
2
“Each stone is about a fingernail wide. Flat on top, not pointy.”
Ring on the hand, her own fingernail right beside the stone, same frame.
3
“It opens at the back — six to eight — so it actually fits.”
Open back, then slide it on.
4
“The big pearl is fifteen millimetres. About a thumbnail.”
Pearl held against her thumbnail. Same rule — both in frame, no cut.
5
“And the chain’s one and a half, so it disappears under a collar.”
Chain against a shirt collar, close. The one measurement where small is the selling point.
6
“Two thousand forty-nine, and fifteen ninety-nine. Free delivery.”
Both worn, daylight. Price chips beside each piece.
Close
“Now you know exactly what’s arriving.”
Straight to camera. 1.2s end card over live footage. SHOP NOW.

Why this one

Size is the one question nobody in this category answers, and 42% of shoppers judge it from the photo alone — which is also the biggest single cause of jewellery returns. This is the script people save and come back to.

Two rules or it fails

The referent must be in the same frame as the piece, and there must be no cut between the object and the thing it is measured against. A cut destroys the proof.

Four things that apply to all three

Check the two numbers against the real pieces before she records. 17mm and 15mm come off the product page, and that same page is wrong about the stones — so verify them, then they are safe to say.

No percentage is spoken in any script. The site currently says Buy 2 = 10%; your brief said 20%. Until that’s settled it goes in the caption, where it changes in ten seconds without a reshoot.

#Ad sits in the top third from frame one, for roughly a third of the runtime. The brand carries the liability, not the creator.

Captions clear the bottom 40% of the frame, and darken the footage behind the words rather than sitting in a grey box.
