I’ll start with what I don’t like

33s · 9:16 · clean room, window light, tripod · both pieces on from frame one
0.0 [no voice] Both pieces already on — ring on the hand, lariat at the neck. She looks down at them, then up. Don’t start static.
0.5 “I’ll start with what I don’t like.” Straight to camera. Level, unbothered. This is the whole reel — nobody in this category opens with a negative, and it buys you the next thirty seconds.
2.6 “The ring is open at the back.” Macro — turn the ring so the open back is clearly visible. Hold it. Don’t hide the thing you just admitted to.
6.0 “That’s how it fits a six to an eight. But you notice it for a day.” Ring going on, hand flexing. The negative and the reason for it, in one breath.
9.6 “And the pearl is a shell pearl. It isn’t pretending to be a sea pearl.” Macro on the baroque drop, turning. Second honest negative. Two is what makes it a review instead of an ad.
13.4 [hard cut — the pivot is the edit, not a line] Cut on the beat to a bright macro. No “but anyway”, no “now the good part”. The picture turns.
13.8 “Two stones that don’t match. A tiger eye and a champagne crystal.” Macro, both stones in one frame.
17.6 “The tiger eye is a real stone. That’s why the light moves on it.” The money shot. Tilt the ring slowly under window light so the sheen physically travels across the band. Hold two full seconds — longer than feels right.
21.6 “And the necklace has a slider — you set how low the pearl falls.” Fingers moving the slider, then two quick cuts at two different lengths.
25.2 “Both plated over surgical steel, two years on the plating.” Both worn together, plain top, daylight.
28.4 “Two thousand forty-nine, and fifteen ninety-nine. Free delivery.” Ring price on the hand shot, necklace price on the neck shot.
31.2 “Necklace I’d buy again. Ring if you like things a bit odd.” Small smile. A real verdict, not a rave — a review that recommends both equally isn’t a review. Then 1.3s end card over live footage, music resolving.

The negatives, the numbers, the rules

Pick the two that are actually true for her

A“The ring is open at the back. That’s how it fits a six to an eight, but you notice it for a day.” Safest and most useful — it doubles as the sizing answer, which is the biggest blocker on buying a ring online.
B“The pearl is a shell pearl. It isn’t pretending to be a sea pearl.” Says the quiet part out loud and lands the brand’s honesty position in one line.
C“The chain is fine — one and a half millimetres. If you want something you can feel, this isn’t it.” True, specific, and it filters out the buyer who’d have returned it.
D“Only the six ships today. Five and seven are made to order.” Only if still true on the day. Check the ring page before she records.

Never say

  • “Solid gold” or “18K gold” — both are 18K gold-tone plated over surgical steel
  • “Pearl” on its own — it is a shell pearl, and saying so is the point of the reel
  • “Waterproof” — the FAQ allows incidental contact only
  • “Only a few left” or “ends tonight”
  • “Link in bio” — it is a paid ad with a real button
#Ad in the top third from frame one, held about ten seconds. A review reel without disclosure is the single most reported format there is.
Three to confirm.  1 · How long has she had them? A review implies ownership. If it is days, have her say “I’ve had these about a week” — a first-impressions review is honest and credible; an implied long-term one is neither. Nothing in this script claims wear time.  2 · Is the tiger eye genuinely a natural stone? The PDP says so and it is the best line in the reel, but it is also the one claim a rival could challenge.  3 · The site says BUY2 = 10%; the brief said 20%. This card uses ten, because that is what the bag applies.
