Actual sizes
Observation open. The one objection nobody in this category answers: how big is it really. Your own BRAND.md puts it at 42% of shoppers judging size from the image alone — and it is the biggest single driver of jewellery returns.

Why these three and not three more of the same. Six creatives are only six if they read as six. A–C opened on reported speech, a silent demonstration and a declaration; D opens on an observation about the medium itself, E on a command not to buy, and F on a confession, mid-action. Each also argues a different thing: D argues size, E argues fit — who it is for, F argues value without ever discounting. None of the six repeats another’s job.
VARIATION D · 27s · 63 WORDS · SAVES

Online, everything is the same size

Observation open · both pieces · the returns killer nobody else answers
0.0[no voice] Top-down on a plain surface: ring and lariat laid flat, a ₹10 coin beside them. Everything sharp, nothing styled. The frame is the argument. #Ad
0.4“Online, everything is the same size.” Voice over the top-down. True of every product photo ever taken, which is why it lands. Beat.
2.9“So here are the actual sizes.” Hand enters and puts the coin down next to the ring. That movement is the whole hook paid off.
5.4“The band’s seventeen millimetres across the inside.” Macro, ring flat, coin in frame. Then the ring going onto the finger so the number becomes real. 17mm INNER · FITS US 6–8
8.7“Each stone is about a fingernail wide.” Ring on the hand, her own fingernail right beside the stone in the same frame. The referent has to be in shot, not described.
11.6“The big pearl’s fifteen millimetres. About a thumbnail.” Baroque drop held against her thumbnail. Same rule — both things in one frame, no cut between them. 15mm BAROQUE SHELL PEARL
15.2“The chain’s one and a half, so it vanishes under a collar.” Chain against a shirt collar, close. The one measurement where small is the selling point. 1.5mm SNAKE CHAIN
20.3“Two thousand forty-nine, and fifteen ninety-nine. Free delivery.” Both worn, daylight. Chips beside each piece. ₹2,049 ₹1,599 FREE DELIVERY · 7-DAY RETURNS
23.6“Now you know what’s arriving.” The closer states the format’s promise. 1.2s end card over live footage. ↓ SHOP NOW
Why D is the most useful of the six. Every other script argues taste. This one argues information — and it is the only one that reduces returns rather than just driving orders. Size is the biggest unanswered question in online jewellery and the category answers it with nothing: the whole competitive set crops every photo to the same frame, which is exactly what the hook points at. It is also the most saveable, because someone deciding between sizes will come back to it. Two production rules make or break it: the referent must be in the same frame, never described, and there must be no cut between the object and the thing it is measured against — a cut destroys the proof.
VARIATION E · 31s · 74 WORDS · COLD

Don’t buy this ring

Direct contradiction · ring only · the lowest-CAC family in the data
0.0[no voice] Ring on, hand held up, nothing else on the hand. She’s looking at it, not at camera. Product on screen in frame one. #Ad
0.4“Don’t buy this ring if you want it to match anything.” Straight to camera. Completely level, no wink. She means it — that is what makes the flip work later.
5.1“The two stones don’t match each other, let alone your earrings.” Cut on “don’t match” to the tightest macro — both stones in frame, different colour, different size, different shape. She is proving her own objection. 18K GOLD-PLATED · SURGICAL STEEL
9.9“It’s not a stacking ring either. It goes on a bare hand.” Wide on the hand — genuinely nothing else on it. That is the brand’s own instruction for this piece, and obeying it on camera is the proof.
15.0“Open at the back, fits six to eight.” Show the open back, then slide it on. The one practical beat in the script. OPEN BACK · FITS US 6–8
18.3“Two thousand forty-nine. Free delivery, seven-day returns.” Worn, whole hand, daylight. Chip beside the piece. ₹2,049 FREE DELIVERY · 7-DAY RETURNS
21.2“So if you want a matching set — genuinely, don’t.” Back to camera. Say it like advice, not like reverse psychology. The moment this reads as a trick, the script dies.
25.2“If you want one odd thing people ask about — this is it.” Small shrug. The flip, and the only sell in thirty-one seconds. 1.2s end card over live footage. ↓ SHOP NOW
Why E is the one I’d put the most cold budget behind. Across 52 DTC accounts, direct contradiction ran 25–40% under a benefit-led baseline on cold prospecting — the largest effect of any hook family measured, and the one A–C left on the table. It works because disqualifying people is the most expensive-looking thing a brand can do, so it reads as confidence rather than salesmanship. The risk is tonal, not legal: if Ananya plays it as a wink, it becomes reverse psychology and the whole thing collapses. She has to mean it. Note the script also obeys the brand’s own styling instruction — “wear it on its own, with the rest of the hand kept bare” — on camera, which is a small piece of proof most brands never bother to give.
VARIATION F · 22s · 50 WORDS · VALUE

I never wear the expensive stuff

Confession, mid-action · both pieces · value without a discount
0.4“I never wear the expensive stuff.” Open mid-action — she is already sorting through an open drawer or a dish of her own jewellery as she says it. Not posed, not to camera yet. Six words, payload on the first one. #Ad
2.9“It lives in here. These two are on me.” Top-down on the drawer — good pieces sitting untouched — then cut up to her hand and neck, both Naira pieces on. The cut is the whole argument.
6.6“The ring’s two stones that don’t match. On purpose.” Macro, both stones, slow rotate. 18K GOLD-PLATED · SURGICAL STEEL
10.9“The necklace has a slider, so it changes length.” Fingers on the slider, one quick length change. SHELL PEARL 2-YEAR PLATING ASSURANCE
14.6“Two thousand forty-nine, and fifteen ninety-nine. Free delivery.” Both worn, daylight. Chips beside each piece. ₹2,049 ₹1,599 FREE DELIVERY · 7-DAY RETURNS
17.8“Buy the one you’ll actually wear.” Straight to camera, closing the drawer. Seven words that make the value case without a percentage anywhere. 1.2s end card over live footage. ↓ SHOP NOW
Why F is the one that survives the offer ending. It argues value without a discount, a comparison or a competitor — the expensive things sit in a drawer, these two are being worn, and the viewer does the arithmetic themselves. That is cost-per-wear made emotional instead of numerical, and it is the only one of the six that still works word-for-word when BUY2 expires. It is also the shortest and cheapest to shoot: one drawer, one cut, two macros. The one thing to get right is that the drawer must be real — her own jewellery, genuinely nicer things, no props and no other brand’s box in frame.

Run E and A against each other on cold — contradiction against objection-first, the two strongest families, same product, same creator. That is a real experiment. B and D go to saves, C and F to warm and festive. Six is roughly a quarter of the way to the twenty-four creatives the published winner rate implies you need.

Never say — all six

  • “Tiger eye”, “natural stone”, “real stone”, “the light moves on it”
  • “Brilliant-cut” — both stones are flat-topped rose cuts
  • “Solid gold” or “18K gold” — 18K gold-tone plated over surgical steel
  • “Pearl” alone — it is a shell pearl
  • “Waterproof” · “only a few left” · “link in bio” · any rival’s name
Every measurement spoken in D comes straight off the product page — 17mm inner diameter, 15mm baroque pearl, 1.5mm chain. Check them against the physical pieces before she records, because the same page is wrong about the stones.
#Ad top third, frame one, a third of each runtime. Brand carries the liability, not the creator.

What none of the six does, and why

None mentions the pairing offer. The site says BUY2 = 10% and the brief said 20%; until that is settled, a spoken percentage is a promise the bag may not keep. It belongs in the caption, where it can be changed in ten seconds without a reshoot.

And none claims wear time. A says two people have already asked, C says she bought them, F says she never wears the expensive things — all three need her to actually mean them. D and E are true on day one regardless, which makes them the safest two to shoot first if the pieces only just arrived.
