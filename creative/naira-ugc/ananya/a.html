IDEA 01 · RING
They don’t match
Cold traffic
Reported speech + craft
“Someone picked up my hand and asked if I’d lost the other stone.”
  • Beat. Then: “No. They’re meant to be different.”
  • Macro on the tiger eye, tilting slowly in window light so the sheen actually travels across it. Hold it longer than feels comfortable — this is the shot.
  • “One’s a natural tiger eye. That’s why it moves like that. The other’s a cut champagne crystal.”
  • “And each one sits in its own bezel — shaped to follow the stone, not to correct it.”
  • Hand doing something ordinary — holding a cup, pushing hair back. “Open back, so it fits six to eight.”
  • “Two thousand and forty-nine. Free delivery, seven-day returns.”
Why it’s a 10. It is reported speech — the family you already picked — carrying a craft argument nobody in Indian demi-fine makes. Everyone else sells matched and symmetrical; this argues asymmetry on purpose, and the line about the bezel following the stone is already on your own PDP and is better than anything I’d write. The chatoyancy is the rarest thing here: a genuinely filmable property no competitor can fake, because most of them don’t use a natural stone. Confirm the tiger eye really is natural before this airs — it is the one claim a rival could challenge.
IDEA 02 · LARIAT
One necklace, three necklaces
Saves & styling
Answers the real blocker
“This is one necklace. I’ve worn it as three.”
  • Macro: fingers on the slider, moving it. “There’s a slider. You decide how far the pearl falls.”
  • Three hard cuts, same piece, ~3s each — long over a white shirt · mid over a kurta · short and high for a dinner. Same necklace, three completely different necklines.
  • Macro on the baroque pearl. “The big one’s a shell pearl, about fifteen millimetres. Baroque just means it isn’t round — so nobody else’s is the same shape as mine.”
  • “Chain’s one and a half millimetres, so it sits flat and doesn’t fight a collar.”
  • “Fifteen ninety-nine. Free delivery.”
Why it’s a 10. A lariat’s real blocker isn’t price or trust — it’s what do I wear it with, and this answers it three times in ten seconds. Styling content is the proven save-heavy format in Indian jewellery, and saves are the second-strongest ranking signal after watch time — well ahead of comments, which have collapsed to around 3%. It also turns the slider from a spec into a reason to buy. Say shell pearl, not pearl — the honesty is free and the alternative is a complaint.
IDEA 03 · BOTH
Nothing here is pretending
The pairing offer
AOV · ₹3,283
“My friend picked up my hand and asked which stone was the real one.”

  • Macro, tiger eye: “This is a natural tiger eye. Actual stone, out of the ground.”
  • Macro, crystal: “This one’s a cut crystal. It isn’t a diamond and nobody ever said it was.”
  • Cut to the necklace: “Same with this. Shell pearl, not a sea pearl. It says so on the page.”
  • Both worn together, plain top, good light. “That’s the bit I actually like. Nothing here is pretending to be something else.”
  • “Ring’s two thousand forty-nine, necklace is fifteen ninety-nine. Take both and the code knocks ten percent off in the bag.”
  • “Free delivery. Seven days to change your mind.”
Why it’s a 10. The category’s central anxiety is is it real, and every competitor answers it by implying yes. This answers it by saying exactly what each stone is — which is the most disarming move available and the one a rival cannot copy without rewriting their own product pages. It is also the natural home for the pairing offer, because it is the only idea that needs both pieces on screen. Pair maths: ₹3,648 → ₹3,283, comfortably above your ₹1,800–2,400 AOV target.
