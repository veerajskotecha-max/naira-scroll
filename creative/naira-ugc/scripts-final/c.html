Why that line works

“Someone asked me where this is from” is not a hook about jewellery. It is reported speech — somebody else said something, and you are about to find out what happened. It praises the product without the brand making a single claim, it opens a story the viewer has to finish, and it puts a third person in the room. All five scripts here are built on it.

01  /  THE PRINCIPLE

Let someone else say it.

Five rejections in, the one line you picked out is the one that does four jobs at once. Here is what it is actually doing, so the other four can do it too.

1 · It is social proof with no claim attached

The brand never says the bracelet looks expensive. A stranger does. Nobody argues with a reported compliment the way they argue with an advertised one — and legally it is bulletproof, because no claim has been made about the product at all.

2 · It opens a story the viewer has to close

Someone asked… demands a what happened next. That is a real curiosity gap, not a manufactured one like “guess the price”. It buys you the four seconds that decide everything.

3 · It is dialogue, in a room with no set

The effectiveness research is blunt that dialogue, characters and a sense of place separate ads that work from ads that don’t — and a clean well-lit room gives you no place at all. Reported speech puts a second person in the frame without a second person on set. It is how you get right-brain advertising out of a tripod and a wall.

4 · It is built to be forwarded

DM shares now carry roughly 3–5× the weight of a like for reaching new people; comments have collapsed to about 3%. In India a Reel gets forwarded when it lets the sender say something about a third person. Every script here has a third person in it.

Where these came from

Four of the five are developed straight out of the hook bank in the original teardown — lines 28 (“four people asked me where this is from, in one day”), 29 (“my mother asked if it was real”), 01 (“I almost didn’t order this”) and 32 (“the one people stop you for”). They were sitting in the bank all along under objection-first and social-proof interception — the two families the CAC data rates highest on cold traffic. I went looking for clever instead.

What the clean room costs, and how these pay it back

A tidy room in good light is the right call — it is how the last reel was shot and the footage was never the problem. But it removes place, which is one of the strongest features an ad can have. These scripts compensate in three ways, and every one of them is free:

  • Reported speech in every opening — a second voice, without a second person.
  • The product is in frame at 0.0 seconds, on her wrist, in every script. The last reel took 4.6 seconds to show anything and 8.2 to show jewellery.
  • The specs live inside the lines, not in chips. On-screen text, hard CTAs and fact stacks measurably lower emotional response. The last reel was wall-to-wall chips.
02  /  THE SCRIPTS

Five, written out.

Clean room, window light, tripod, Western styling, sleeve pushed back. All ~2.7 words a second. Delivery note for all five: tell it, don’t perform it. She is repeating something that happened, to one friend, and she is slightly amused by it.

01Your pick, built out
I remember
27s · reported speech + withholding · the one you chose
0.0[no voice] Mid-motion — she pushes her sleeve back and settles the bracelet on her wrist. Product on screen in frame one. Don't start static.
0.6“Someone asked me where this is from.” To camera. Like she's telling one friend, not an audience.
3.4“And I told her I couldn't remember.” Small shrug. Entirely unbothered.
5.8[beat — hold it one count longer than feels right] She looks straight down the lens. Nothing moves.
6.6“I remember.” Cut on the word to macro: the wrist turning, the paperclip links, the pavé one catching. This cut is the joke — land it on the word, not after.
8.2“I've just been enjoying being the only one who does.” Stay in macro. Let the metal do the talking while she says it.
11.6“It's the Bold Nocturne. It's from Naira.” Back to her. First and only time the brand is spoken.
14.4“18K gold-plated over surgical steel, anti-tarnish sealed.” Macro, slow rotate. One soft key, one small hard glint — without the glint gold reads as plastic.
18.0“Lobster clasp, little extender, so it fits anyone.” One-handed clasp click in close-up, with the live sound.
21.2“Nineteen ninety-nine. One-fifty to deliver it.” Worn, whole arm, natural light. Price chip beside the wrist, not in a corner.
24.0“Seven-day returns. Go on. Take it.” Small smile. She gives in. Then 1.3s end card over live footage, music resolving.
Why it works. The whole ad is one small confession — she was gatekeeping, and she's decided to stop. That turns the price reveal into a surrender rather than a pitch, which is the most disarming way to say a number out loud. “I remember” is the best two words in anything I've written for you, and the cut lands on them. 61 words.
02
She asked if it was real
26s · reported speech + the hardest possible critic
0.0[no voice] Already wearing it. She turns her wrist over, once, looking at it.
0.5“My mum picked up my wrist at a wedding and said — ‘ye asli hai?’ ” To camera. The quoted bit in her mother's voice, not her own. That switch is the whole performance.
5.0“Which, from her, is a compliment.” Dry. Beat.
7.4“She has been buying gold for forty years. She was not being polite.” Macro on the links, turning slowly.
12.0“It's not gold. It's 18K gold-plated over surgical steel.” Stay in macro. Saying it isn't gold is the strongest thing in this script — own it, don't hurry past it.
16.0“Anti-tarnish sealed, nickel-free, two years on the plating.” Clasp click with the sound, then worn on the wrist.
20.2“Nineteen ninety-nine, one-fifty delivered, seven-day returns.” Whole arm, natural light, price chip beside the piece.
24.2“I still haven't told her.” Small smile, straight to camera. End card over live footage.
Why it works. Your mother is the most sceptical jewellery buyer you will ever meet, and if she can't tell, nobody can. It kills the trust objection with a witness instead of an argument — and because she volunteers that it isn't real gold, the brand never has to defend anything. The last line is the forward: every woman sends this to the friend whose mother is the same. Highest share potential of the five.
03
I almost didn't order it
26s · objection-first — the lowest-CAC family on cold traffic
0.0[no voice] Wearing it. Sleeve pushed back. She's looking at the camera before she speaks.
0.4“I almost didn't order this.” Flat. Honest. No smile yet.
2.4“Two thousand rupees for something that looks like this felt wrong.” Macro: the bracelet, generous, unhurried. Let the viewer agree with her.
7.0“Like there had to be a catch.” Back to her. Beat.
9.4“The catch is that it's plated. It's not solid gold and it never said it was.” Straight to camera. Completely level. This is the line the whole ad rests on.
14.8“18K over surgical steel, anti-tarnish sealed, two years on the plating.” Macro: links, then the clasp closing with the live click.
19.6“Nineteen ninety-nine. One-fifty delivered. Seven days to send it back.” Worn, whole arm, daylight.
24.0“I've worn it every day since.” Only if true. If not, use: “I ordered the chain the week after.” End card over live footage.
Why it works. Across 52 DTC accounts, objection-first hooks ran 15–25% lower CAC than benefit-led ones on cold traffic — and at ₹3,000/day you are almost entirely cold. This is the only script of the five that names the catch out loud, which is exactly why it is believed. Run this one at cold audiences.
04
She thought it was a gift
24s · reported speech + the self-purchase turn
0.0[no voice] Wearing both pieces. She's adjusting the bracelet as she starts talking.
0.4“Someone at work said — ‘oh, who gave you that?’ ” To camera, reporting it. Light.
3.4“Nobody gave me that.” Beat. Flat.
5.2“I gave me that.” Cut on the word to macro — the wrist turning. Land the cut on “me”.
7.0“On a Tuesday. For no reason.” Stay macro. Slow.
9.6“It's nineteen ninety-nine, which is roughly one dinner out.” Back to her, wearing it, entirely normal.
13.8“18K gold-plated over surgical steel. Two years on the plating.” Clasp click, macro, live sound.
18.0“One-fifty to deliver, seven days to change your mind.” Whole arm, daylight.
21.6“Stop waiting for someone to buy you jewellery.” Straight to camera. Warm, not preachy. End card over live footage.
Why it works. It sits on a verified shift in how this customer buys — demi-fine is bought as self-celebration, not received as a gift. “I gave me that” is the most forwardable line in the document: it is a small flag planted, and women send that to each other. The cost comparison does the price work without a discount. Best festive and gifting-season cut.
05
Nobody has ever asked me what it cost
28s · the inversion — kills price anxiety without mentioning price
0.0[no voice] Wearing it. Wrist up, turning it in the window light.
0.4“I've worn this to an office, two weddings and a funeral.” To camera. Matter-of-fact. Adjust the list to what is actually true.
4.6“Not one person has asked me what it cost.” Beat.
7.6“They ask where it's from. Every single time.” Macro: links turning, the pavé catching.
11.0“That's the actual thing nobody tells you about jewellery.” Back to her.
14.4“Nobody is doing the maths. They're just looking.” Three fast cuts — same bracelet, three different sleeves. Shoot in ten minutes.
18.0“18K gold-plated over surgical steel. Anti-tarnish sealed.” Macro, clasp click with the live sound.
21.6“Nineteen ninety-nine. One-fifty delivered. Seven-day returns.” Worn, whole arm, daylight. Price chip beside the piece.
25.6“Nobody's going to ask. Tap Shop Now.” End card over live footage.
Why it works. This is the smartest of the five. The real barrier at this price is not will it tarnish — it is will people be able to tell. That is a social fear, and social fear is what gets shared. The script answers it by pointing out something true and slightly liberating: nobody is auditing your wrist. It reframes price anxiety out of existence and it makes the versatility case. Highest save potential — this is the one people send to themselves.
03  /  THE CLEAN-ROOM SPEC

Eleven things the last reel did.

Same setup as the Riya reel — a tidy room in good light. The footage was never the problem. These are the eleven measured faults that made it a 5.4, and what to do instead. Fixing this list is worth more than the script choice.

Lighting, since this is the whole reason for a clean room

  • One window, side-on, subject 1–1.5m from it. White card or a sheet of A4 on the shadow side to lift the fill.
  • For macros, add one small hard glint — a phone torch bounced off something, or a second window. One soft key alone renders gold as matte plastic; the kicker is what makes it read as metal.
  • Never the built-in flash. If adding light, CRI ≥95 — cheap LEDs put a green spike into gold that no grade removes. And clean the bracelet before every take: at macro a fingerprint is the whole shot.
  • Face on the tripod, macros handheld with a slight drift — stillness reads as credibility on a face, movement makes metal live. Eyeline on the upper third (y ≈ 640) so the lower frame stays clear for captions.
Sound — decide before the shoot, not in the edit

Every currently-trending Indian audio is unusable in a paid ad. Instagram listing a sound does not mean Meta licensed it, and a business account can't reach the full library anyway. Use Meta Sound Collection (cleared, but Meta platforms only) or Epidemic / Artlist if it also has to run elsewhere. Deliver at −14 LUFS, −1.0 dBTP. And turn off Advantage+ Music and Image animation at ad level — Meta enables them by default and will lay an unbriefed bed over the voice.

04  /  HOW TO RUN IT

Three cuts, one afternoon.

The recommendation

Shoot 01, 03 and 05 in the same session. They share every setup — same room, same light, same wardrobe, same macros. The only thing that changes is what she says to camera. Budget an extra twenty minutes per script, not an extra shoot.

01 “I remember” is the one you already liked and the one to lead with. 03 “I almost didn't order it” goes to cold traffic, because objection-first is the family with the lowest measured CAC there. 05 “Nobody has ever asked me what it cost” is the one people save, and the one I'd put money on outlasting the other two.

Hold 02 and 04 for the festive window — both are gift-adjacent and both will read better in October than in September.

Nine setups, five talking-head takes. One afternoon, and you leave with three finished ads and the parts for two more.

Then test them properly

Four cells, one body each, everything else held constant, 5–7 days. Kill on hook rate under 20% at 5,000 impressions or outbound CTR under 0.6% at 8,000 — never before 1,000 impressions or 48 hours. Use hook rate to kill, never to pick: no published research links thumbstop to ROAS, and an ad can hold a 45% hook rate and sell nothing. Name them NAIRA_SEP26_SEJAL_HOOK-REMEMBER_27s_9x16_v1 and keep the hook label identical in the ad name, the file name and the UTM.

Non-negotiable, all five

Never “waterproof” · never “18K gold” — it is 18K gold-plated over surgical steel, and script 02 and 03 say so out loud, which is a strength · any spoken price carries the ₹150 delivery · #Ad in the top third from frame one, held a third of the runtime · no named competitor · no false urgency · and every “someone said” has to be something someone actually said. The whole family rests on reported speech being real — invent one and the format stops working the moment anyone asks.
