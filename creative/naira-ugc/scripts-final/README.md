# Five scripts — reported speech (Sejal)

`../NAIRA_Five-Scripts_Reported-Speech_Sejal.pdf` — 11 pages. Five complete scripts with
timecodes, plus the clean-room production spec.

## The one signal that produced these

After several rejected hooks, the client picked out a single line as good:

> *"Someone asked me where this is from…"*

That line is not a hook about jewellery. It is **reported speech** — someone else said something,
and the viewer is about to find out what happened. It does four things at once:

1. **Social proof with no claim attached.** The brand never says the bracelet looks expensive; a
   stranger does. Legally bulletproof, because no product claim is made at all.
2. **Opens a story the viewer has to close.** A real curiosity gap, unlike a manufactured
   "guess the price".
3. **It is dialogue, in a room with no set.** Character, dialogue and place are what separate ads
   that work from ads that don't — and a clean room offers no place. Reported speech puts a second
   person in frame without a second person on set.
4. **Built to be forwarded.** DM shares carry ~3–5× the weight of a like; comments have fallen to
   ~3%. In India a Reel gets forwarded when it lets the sender say something about a third person.

**All five scripts are built on it.**

## Where they came from

Four of the five develop lines that were already in the hook bank in
`../NAIRA_UGC_Teardown_Hooks_VO-Reel_Plan.pdf` — numbers 28, 29, 01 and 32, filed under
*objection-first* and *social-proof interception*, the two families the CAC data rates highest on
cold traffic. They were there from the start; earlier rounds went looking for clever instead.

## The five

| | Script | Family | Length | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | **I remember** | reported speech + withholding | 27s | Lead — the client's pick |
| 02 | She asked if it was real | the hardest possible critic | 26s | Festive; highest share potential |
| 03 | I almost didn't order it | objection-first | 26s | **Cold traffic** — lowest measured CAC family |
| 04 | She thought it was a gift | the self-purchase turn | 24s | Festive / gifting window |
| 05 | Nobody has ever asked me what it cost | the inversion | 28s | Highest save potential |

**Recommendation:** shoot 01, 03 and 05 in one session — they share every setup, and only the
talking-head take changes. Hold 02 and 04 for October.

Script 05 is the sharpest idea of the five: the real barrier at this price is not *will it tarnish*
but *will people be able to tell*. That is a social fear, and social fear is what gets shared. It
answers it by pointing out that nobody is auditing your wrist.

## Shoot setup

Clean room, window light, tripod — the Riya reel setup, which was never the problem. Western
styling per the reference photo, sleeve pushed back, gold against bare arm.

The PDF's §03 lists the **eleven measured faults** that made the Riya reel a 5.4 and what to do
instead: motion in frame one, product on the wrist at 0.0s, caption band fixed at y 1150–1330,
licensed music with no digital silence, live clasp foley, locked AE/AF for macros, one white
balance, plain wall, one caption style, price chip beside the product, and a 1.3s end card over
live footage. **Fixing that list is worth more than the script choice.**

## Hard rule specific to this family

**Every "someone said" has to be something someone actually said.** The format rests on the
reported speech being real; invent one and it stops working the moment anyone asks. Three lines are
flagged conditional in the PDF: "I've worn it every day since" (03), the venue list in 05, and the
wedding in 02.
